Rules of thumb

If you hear…It's probably…
An augmented-second lunge right at the startHicaz — nothing else here has it
Plain minor-key color, no “in-between” notesNihavend
Major-like brightness with a softened third, ending on G with total reposeRast
A-tonic, bent second degree, gravity pulling to D, modest rangeUşşak
A-tonic, bent second, but entering high with a bright sixth and E as the pivotHüseyni

Where to go from here

DirectionSuggestions
More makams Natural next steps: Segâh and Hüzzam (tonic on the segâh note itself), Kürdîli Hicazkâr (Nihavend's flat-side cousin), Saba (with its haunting shrunken fourth), and Nikriz (Rast's tonic with a Hicaz middle).
Real repertoire Follow the listening lists in Chapters 5–9; TRT archive scores at divanmakam.com and neyzen.com let you read along with recordings.
Rhythm Learn the usul system properly — start by clapping düm-tek patterns of sofyan (4) and aksak (9 = 2+2+2+3) along with songs you now recognize.
Deeper theory Signell's Makam: Modal Practice in Turkish Art Music is the classic English-language study; for the tuning debates, look up Ozan Yarman's 79-tone work.
Final advice Theory told you where the notes are; only repertoire can tell you why they matter. Pick one song per makam from the chapters, listen daily for a week, and hum the cadences. The makams will move from your notes into your ear — which is where they have always lived.
