Chapter 02The Comma: Tuning & Intervals

Turkish theory divides the octave into 53 tiny, equal steps called commas (koma). Every interval in this course is measured in commas. Master this one idea and the rest of makam theory becomes arithmetic.

53 steps in an octave

In the standard system — named Arel–Ezgi–Uzdilek (AEU) after the three theorists who codified it — the octave contains 53 commas. One comma is about 22.6 cents, roughly a quarter of a Western semitone. Why 53? Because a chain of acoustically pure fifths very nearly closes after 53 steps: it is the smallest equal division of the octave that represents Pythagorean tuning almost perfectly. The familiar intervals come out as:

IntervalCommasFor comparison (12-tone West)
Octave5312 semitones = 53 commas
Perfect fifth317 semitones ≈ 30.9 commas
Perfect fourth225 semitones ≈ 22.1 commas
Whole tone (major second)92 semitones ≈ 8.8 commas

The difference between the 9-comma and 8-comma step is exactly the kind of nuance that makes makam melody glow: the 8-comma second feels a shade “softer,” and it is the interval that produces the beloved segâh note in Rast and Uşşak.

Four different “B”s

Here is the practical consequence of comma thinking. Between A and B-natural, Turkish music has several distinct, named pitches. Checkpoint A makam scale must add up. Every tetrachord spans a perfect fourth = 22 commas; every pentachord spans a perfect fifth = 31 commas; the octave is 53. When you see a scale written as T–K–S–T + T–K–S, verify it: (9+8+5+9) + (9+8+5) = 31 + 22 = 53. ✓

Theory grid vs. living intonation

AEU is a map, not the territory. Measurements of master performers show, for example, that the Uşşak second degree is typically sung 2–3 commas below B, not the theoretical 1 — and that it slides expressively. Other theoretical models (24-tone Pythagorean, 79-tone systems) try to capture this better. None of this should worry you now: the AEU grid is what sheet music uses, what teachers teach first, and a fine scaffold for your ear. This site's audio is exact AEU.
